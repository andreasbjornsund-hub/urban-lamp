What makes a smartphone "Zone 1 certified"?

Zone 1 is the second most hazardous explosive atmosphere classification. IEC 60079-10 defines it as an area where an explosive gas atmosphere is likely to occur in normal operation, occasionally. Think pump rooms on chemical tankers, gas processing areas in refineries, or classified zones in LNG terminals. See also: ATEX and IECEx explained and the guide to hazardous area classification.

A device certified for Zone 1 must meet a recognised protection concept under ATEX or IECEx. For portable electronics this usually means intrinsic safety (Ex ia or Ex ib): the device cannot release enough electrical or thermal energy to ignite a surrounding explosive atmosphere, even in fault conditions. Some Ex cases use a flameproof enclosure (Ex db) instead.

Not all "rugged" phones are Zone 1 certified. Military-spec ruggedness (MIL-STD-810) and IP68 water resistance have nothing to do with Zone 1 certification. A phone can be fully waterproof and survive a 1.5 m drop but still be prohibited in your gas processing area.
